Chapter 1 
Introduction 
The Accounting Module is the financial heart of the ERP system. It records, classifies, 
summarizes, and reports every financial transaction generated throughout the system. Rather 
than requiring accountants to manually re-enter information from sales, purchases, payroll, 
inventory, or banking, the Accounting Module automatically receives approved transactions 
from integrated modules, converts them into balanced accounting entries, and updates the 
company's financial records in real time. 
The module is designed to support businesses of all sizes—from small enterprises to large 
organizations—by providing accurate financial information for operational decision-making, 
statutory reporting, tax compliance, and management analysis. 
Unlike the Point of Sale (POS), which focuses on processing sales efficiently, the Accounting 
Module focuses on the financial impact of those sales. Every sale, purchase, payment, receipt, 
inventory movement, or adjustment ultimately becomes part of the company's accounting 
records through this module. 
The Accounting Module follows the principles of double-entry accounting, ensuring that every 
financial transaction maintains the accounting equation: 
Assets = Liabilities + Equity 
Every posted transaction must always satisfy: 
Total Debits = Total Credits 
This rule is enforced by the system and cannot be bypassed. 
 
1.1 Objectives of the Accounting Module 
The Accounting Module is designed to achieve the following objectives: 

===== PAGE 2 =====

 Maintain complete and accurate accounting records.  
 Automatically capture transactions from integrated ERP modules.  
 Eliminate duplicate data entry.  
 Ensure compliance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) and 
applicable local accounting regulations.  
 Support Rwanda Revenue Authority (RRA) tax requirements and EBM integration.  
 Generate real-time financial statements and management reports.  
 Strengthen internal controls through approvals, audit trails, and user permissions.  
 Improve business decision-making through timely financial information.  
 Reduce human error by automating accounting postings.  
 
1.2 Core Principles 
The Accounting Module is built around the following principles: 
1. Automation First 
Users should not manually record transactions that already originate from another module. 
For example: 
 Sales recorded in POS automatically generate accounting entries.  
 Supplier bills automatically create Accounts Payable entries.  
 Inventory movements automatically update inventory valuation.  
 Customer payments automatically reduce outstanding receivables.  
2. Single Source of Truth 
Information should only be entered once. 
Example: 
A cashier records a sale in the POS. 
The accountant should not enter the same sale again. 
Instead, the Accounting Module automatically receives the transaction. 
 
3. Real-Time Accounting 
Financial reports are updated immediately after transactions are posted. 

===== PAGE 3 =====

Managers can therefore monitor: 
 Current cash position  
 Current profit  
 Outstanding customer balances  
 Outstanding supplier balances  
 Inventory value  
 VAT payable  
 Business performance  
without waiting until month-end. 
4. Complete Auditability 
Every accounting transaction must be traceable. 
The system records: 
 Who created it  
 When it was created  
 Who modified it  
 Previous values  
 Current values  
 Source module  
 Source document  
Nothing is ever silently overwritten.

1.4 Position of Accounting Within the ERP

The Accounting Module acts as the central financial engine of the ERP. 
Every operational module communicates with Accounting. 
 
 
1.5 Integration with the Existing POS 
The Point of Sale (POS) module already manages customer sales and invoice generation. The 
Accounting Module extends this process by automatically recording the financial consequences 
of each completed sale. 
This architecture ensures that 
Accounting becomes the 
financial destination of all 
business activities. 
 

===== PAGE 5 =====

The integration follows an event-driven approach: 
1. A cashier completes a sale in the POS.  
2. The POS validates the transaction and issues an invoice.  
3. If required, the invoice is transmitted to RRA EBM.  
4. The transaction is sent to the Accounting Integration Engine.  
5. The system generates the appropriate journal entries.  
6. Customer balances, tax accounts, inventory, and the General Ledger are updated 
automatically.  
7. Financial reports are refreshed in real time.  
The user does not need to re-enter any accounting information. 
1.6 Accounting Integration Engine 
The Accounting Integration Engine is responsible for converting operational transactions into 
accounting records. 
Its responsibilities include: 
 Validating incoming transactions.  
 Determining the correct debit and credit accounts.  
 Applying tax rules.  
 Posting journal entries.  
 Updating subsidiary ledgers.  
 Updating the General Ledger.  
 Logging audit information.  
 Returning posting status to the originating module.  
If posting fails, the transaction remains visible in an exception queue for review by an authorized 
accountant. 
1.7 Module Independence 
Although integrated with the ERP, the Accounting Module has its own dedicated workspace and 
navigation. 
Users can perform accounting tasks independently without accessing the POS interface. 
This separation improves security, usability, and role-based access control while ensuring 
seamless information flow between operational and financial processes.

SEGMENT 7

7.1 Introduction 
The Bank Reconciliation Module is designed to compare the organization's accounting records 
with official bank statements and identify any differences between the two balances. 
Throughout daily operations, transactions may be recorded in the ERP before they appear on the 
bank statement, or they may appear on the bank statement before they are recorded in the 
accounting system. The reconciliation process ensures that these timing differences, bank 
charges, interest income, direct deposits, standing orders, returned cheques, and other 
adjustments are properly identified and recorded. 
The module provides accountants with a guided reconciliation process that automatically 
matches transactions, highlights unmatched items, and generates reconciliation reports for 
approval. 
A bank account should be reconciled regularly to ensure the accuracy of cash balances, 
strengthen internal controls, and improve the reliability of financial reporting.

SEGMENT 8 
JOURNAL ENTRIES 
 
8.1 Introduction 
The Journal Entries Module is the central posting engine of the Accounting Module. It records 
every financial transaction using the principles of double-entry accounting and serves as the 
primary source of data for the General Ledger, Trial Balance, and Financial Statements. 
Journal entries may be generated automatically by integrated ERP modules or created manually 
by authorized accountants. Automatic journal entries originate from operational activities such as 
sales, purchases, inventory movements, payroll, banking transactions, tax calculations, and fixed 
asset depreciation. Manual journal entries are used for adjustments, corrections, accruals, 
prepayments, provisions, and other accounting events that cannot be generated automatically. 
Every journal entry must be balanced before posting, ensuring that the total debit amount always 
equals the total credit amount. Once posted, journal entries immediately update the General 
Ledger and all related financial reports.

SEGMENT 10 
TRIAL BALANCE 
 
10.1 Introduction 
The Trial Balance Module provides a summarized view of all General Ledger account balances 
at a specific date or accounting period. It is automatically generated from the General Ledger and 
serves as the primary tool for verifying the mathematical accuracy of accounting records before 
preparing financial statements. 
The Trial Balance lists every ledger account together with its debit or credit balance. When 
accounting records are correct, the total debit balances must equal the total credit balances. Any 
imbalance indicates that accounting errors exist and must be investigated before financial 
statements can be finalized. 
The Trial Balance is updated automatically whenever journal entries are posted, reversed, or 
adjusted, ensuring that accountants always have access to current financial balances. 
The module supports real-time reporting, comparative analysis, multiple accounting periods, 
branch reporting, project reporting, and cost centre reporting.

SEGMENT 19

19.1 Introduction 
The Multi-Currency Accounting Module enables organizations to record, process, and report 
financial transactions in multiple currencies while maintaining accurate accounting records in the 
organization's functional currency. 
The module automatically converts foreign currency transactions using configured exchange 
rates, records exchange gains and losses, performs currency revaluations at period-end, and 
generates financial statements in both the functional currency and selected reporting currencies. 
Every module within the ERP—including Sales, Purchases, Banking, Inventory, Payroll, Fixed 
Assets, and General Ledger—is fully integrated with the Multi-Currency Accounting Module, 
ensuring that foreign currency transactions are processed consistently throughout the system. 
The ERP maintains both the original transaction currency and the converted functional currency 
amount, providing complete traceability and compliance with international accounting standards.
